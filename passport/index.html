peakst8 Passport

The whole race week, in one booking.

A Passport is an experience built around an event. The athlete books the event ticket, a place to stay, food, local transfers, recovery and the extras around it in one go: one or two curated base packages, optional paid add-ons on top, one payment.

1. What it is

A normal peakst8 event sells a ticket. A Passport sells an entire package around the event: ticket, stay, food, transport, recovery and extras.

Event ticketThe reason to go

HYROX Noida race entry, a trek permit, a camp place.

StayNear the venue

A private villa or a 5-star hotel, with the room type stated up front.

FoodSorted

Food at the villa, or meals as per the hotel room plan.

TransportGetting around

Local transfers and a venue visit before race weekend.

RecoveryBefore and after

Morning yoga, a carb-loading get-together, a recovery party.

Add-onsMake it yours

Optional, paid: a heritage tour, a room to yourself, an extra night.

Passports come in three groups: Race Week Curation (HYROX, Ironman, UTMB, La Vuelta, the World Marathon Majors), Into the Wild (Himalayan treks) and Training Camps (Kalaripayattu, open-water swimming, Muay Thai, surfing and similar).

Where the event ticket comes from

Some events, such as HYROX, sell their tickets only through their own official channels, not through peakst8. peakst8 cannot sell those tickets directly, so the ticket reaches the athlete as part of a package:

1 · peakst8 buysThe event tickets

From the event's official channel.

2 · peakst8 buildsA Passport around them

Ticket plus stay, food, transport, recovery and add-ons.

3 · The athlete buysThe Passport on peakst8

The ticket is part of the package, not sold on its own.

This is why the booking form asks for what the official ticket needs: name as per government ID, date of birth, gender, HYROX category and an emergency contact. For events peakst8 runs itself, the ticket is simply part of the package.

2. What it solves

Athletes who travel for a race plan everything around it themselves, and peakst8 sells only the ticket. A Passport sorts the whole trip in one booking.

WhoTodayWhat it costs them
Athlete travelling for a raceBooks the entry on one site, then the hotel, transfers, food and recovery separatelyHours of planning, a stay far from the venue, a race week alone. International athletes have it hardest.
Athlete looking for their next challengeNo single place to discover races, treks and camps worth travelling forThey find out late, or not at all
Clubs and gyms bringing a groupThe coordinator builds the trip by hand (a Bali gym wants to bring 10–12 athletes to HYROX Noida)Effort, and no group benefits
peakst8Sells the ticket, nothing around it, and cannot sell tickets at all for events like HYROX that sell only through their own channelsMisses the larger spend around each race

The bet (from the go-to-market plan): some people pick the challenge first and plan the trip around it, and they will pay a premium of about 10–20% for convenience, access and community. This is a hypothesis; the discovery calendar, the waitlists and HYROX Noida bookings test it.

Product philosophy: 1–2 thoughtfully curated base packages per experience, with meaningful optional add-ons (single occupancy, cultural or heritage tours, special dinners with athletes or coaches, recovery and wellness, extra nights or transfers). It scales without turning every booking into custom travel planning.

4. The booking flow

Everything happens on one page, /passport/:id, in five steps. Each step reuses part of the normal event registration.

1 · PackageChoose your base

Pick a package. Each package remembers its own add-ons and answers.

2 · Add-onsMake it yours

Tick the extras. The bar shows the new total before GST.

3 · DetailsWho's coming

The event's registration form: name, contact, ID, race category, diet, emergency contact.

4 · ReviewCheck it

Stay, add-ons, answers and the full price breakdown.

5 · WaiverAgree and pay

The package's or event's waiver. Sign in by email code if needed, then Razorpay.

After payment the athlete lands on the ticket. Closing Razorpay or a failed payment returns them to the Passport page. One booking is for one guest.

5. Paying in other currencies (Razorpay)

Today every Passport is priced and charged in rupees. Paying in the athlete's own currency is being built so international buyers, starting with the Bali group, can pay in their currency.

Today

  • Prices are set in INR. The Razorpay order is created in INR, and checkout opens in INR.
  • The frontend already has a currency menu (INR, USD, EUR, AED) and a currency guess from the device's time zone, but both are switched off ("only rupees for now"). They use fixed, indicative rates (₹88 per US dollar, ₹102 per euro, ₹24 per dirham), so they must not be switched on as they are.

Being built

Backend work on branch feat/razorpay-multi-currency, not merged:

  1. Which currency. The app can ask for a specific currency or "auto". Auto picks one from the athlete's country (Cloudflare's country header). An unsupported country, or an older app that asks for nothing, gets INR as today.
  2. Prices stay in rupees. The organiser's price is in INR. It is converted at a live rate (open.er-api.com, cached for 6 hours). If the rate can't be fetched, the athlete pays in INR.
  3. A 4% conversion margin. Razorpay charges about 3% plus GST on international cards (2% for domestic) and converts at its bank's rate. The foreign-currency total is the rupee total × rate × 1.04, rounded up, so the rupees we receive stay close to the rupee price. The quote shows the margin as its own line.
  4. Checkout in the athlete's currency. The Razorpay order is created in that currency. Razorpay settles to peakst8 in INR.
  5. Records. The rate is locked when the order is created. Each payment stores its currency, the amount in that currency, the rate and the price breakdown. The rupee price stays the price of record for invoices, payouts and reports. A registration keeps its currency for later payments (after approval, retries).

Currencies (25): INR, USD, EUR, GBP, AED, SAR, QAR, OMR, KWD, BHD, SGD, MYR, THB, HKD, JPY, AUD, NZD, CAD, CHF, SEK, NOK, DKK, ZAR, LKR, NPR. Three-decimal currencies (OMR, KWD, BHD) and zero-decimal JPY are handled.

6. How it's built

A Passport is a peakst8 event with extra content. No new checkout: packages are ticket categories, and add-ons hang off packages.

Passport wordWhat it is in peakst8Where it comes from
PassportAn event marked as a Passport, with its group, itinerary, what's included and what's not includedGET /event/:id
PackageA ticket category marked as a package, with images, stay, occupancy and its add-onsGET /event/:id/categories
Add-onA priced extra that can belong to several packages; a registration stores the ones chosenOn the package
Price quoteBase − coupon + add-ons + GSTGET /event/category/:categoryId/cost-break-down
BookingThe normal register or request-approval call, with the chosen add-on idsPOST /registration/:categoryId/register
DiscoveryFeatured Passports, and the rest groupedGET /passport/featured, GET /passport/non-featured

Frontend: peakst8-frontend, app/(routes)/(public)/passport/[id], components/passport, and the Discover packages tab in components/events. Backend: peakst8-backend, src/*/passport.*, and the Passport, Package and Addon models in prisma/schema.prisma.

7. Rules

  • Listing. A Passport is listed while it is public and has not ended. Featured Passports also need registration open (or sold out, so they can show "Sold out"). Order: the team's order, then start date.
  • Pricing. Package price + chosen add-ons, then the coupon, then GST at the club's rate on everything, unless the package is GST-inclusive.
  • Add-ons must belong to the chosen package; otherwise the quote and the booking are refused. One of each per booking.
  • Approval-based packages store the chosen add-ons and charge for them at approval, at the add-on prices on that day.
  • Capacity is the package's ticket capacity, held while the athlete pays. Add-ons have no limit. One booking per athlete per Passport.
  • Duration ("4 Days 3 Nights") is counted from the dates in the venue's time zone.
  • Free total: the ticket is issued straight away, with no Razorpay.

9. Journeys and done criteria

  1. As an athlete, I find HYROX Noida from a poster and book the hotel package with Heritage Day. Done when: the QR lands on Discover packages, the Passport page shows both packages, the review total equals the Razorpay charge, and the ticket is issued.
  2. As an athlete, I join the waitlist for UTMB. Done when: my details are saved against that Passport and I see the confirmation.
  3. As an athlete in Bali, I pay in my currency. Done when: the price shows in my currency on every step, Razorpay opens in that currency for the same amount, and peakst8 receives close to the rupee price. (Not live.)
  4. As an athlete with a pending payment, I come back to finish. Done when: my package and add-ons are locked and "Complete Payment" opens Razorpay for the same booking.
